# E167 — The Greatest Prison

## Summary
The second consecutive session without a die rolled in anger, and the one where the party walks
willingly into the most beautiful cage in the campaign. Picking up mid-encirclement from E166's
cliffhanger, the thirty **Gatewatch** soldiers who met the party's landing never lower a spear — they
simply refuse to move or speak until every weapon is surrendered. **Noctis** rolls a 24 to compare
the guards' sapphire cloaks with his own and learns both are **of Ziven make**; the guards' carry
**moons, stars and a repeated gateway crest** his does not, and the party finally connects — to Jon's
open delight, two years late — that Noctis's cloak is named for **Izzdar the Undying**, the Hive
archmage whose vault they looted fifty sessions ago. The standoff over "all of your weapons" turns
absurd and then quietly menacing: **Quinton's lute** and **Noctis's staff** count as weapons, **Noctis**
plants a hand in a guard's chest to stop him and **loses a strength contest to a 22**, and the guard
removes the lute *"softly but forcibly,"* an *"impossibly unyielding force"* — no aggression, no
possibility of resistance. Their escort names himself **Vaelor Sythran, Gate Watch
Commander**, and walks them through a city that is the session's real
antagonist: broad avenues, pale stone veined with glowing gold and purple, **no beggars, no shouting
merchants, nobody drunk**, thousands of drow who are each *"the best version of what they
represent."* **"It's too perfect. It's almost odd."** Overhead drift the **inactive portals**. The
crowd's reaction is the tell — a mother says *"material travelers, it's been decades,"* an old man
guesses *"another fractured gate"* — the citizens **speculate** the party is from another world but
**do not know for sure**, and the guards **do not comment** either way (Jon, E167 review).
**Berrian's Divine Sense** finds no celestials,
fiends or undead in the crowd. They are logged by name at a reception desk and walked down many
flights of black obsidian stairs into the **Quiet Vaults**, cells deep inside the floating mountain
the city rides on — clean, spacious, monastic, with beds, a wash basin, lanterns and **a small
library, all of it in Drow**. Their neighbours are two Githyanki and **four caged Slaad**. The
Githyanki are the session's gift: **Taarik Vosh**, a merchant jailed for unloading cargo before
customs, and **Rhen Sarik**, a meditating **cartographer** arrested trying to steal better maps from a
restricted observatory called **Talmaq** *(spelling unconfirmed)*. Rhen Sarik delivers the cosmology
E166 only gestured at —
**"the god is the isle"**: the gods sundered and sent here *slept, and in sleeping created an entire
ecosystem of themselves*, so the God Isles are not places gods live but the gods themselves, drifting.
He confirms **Melora** is known and here, which lands hard on Berrian; he confirms **Bahamut's**
mountains have trees and treasure; and a religion check from **Cruucar** and **Berrian** (18 apiece)
gets Jon to confirm **Tiamat is also here**. He also hands over the line the episode is named for —
a Ziven saying about their own cities: **"The greatest prison ever built is one where the prisoners no
longer remember they are imprisoned."** And he reveals that **Captain Kael'vorr** is famous, that one
of his monikers is **the Curse of the Slaad**, and that he broke the great **Slaad uprising** and drove
them out of **Tu'narath**. Torgoth is mid-attempt at speaking **Tongues** to the caged Slaad when the
guards return. The party is taken to the **Hall of Verity** — a severe black amphitheatre with a single
table at its centre — to face **Examiner Caelith Vorell, Senior Inquisitor of Ilharess**:
violet eyes that never blink, six chairs conjured with a gesture, tea conjured with a
snap as *"a formal courtesy,"* and a self-writing slate tablet taking the minutes. He asks three
words — **Names. Origins. Purpose.** — accepts the answers, listens to the whole Rift-and-Vault-of-Izdar
story, and then closes his ledger: **"You have been forthcoming. And yet there are truths you keep
hidden. You intend not to share. However, this matter exceeds my authority."** Back in the cells the
**Slaad are simply gone** — *"their sentence was carried out."* Then the real interview: **High
Examiner Aethon Valis, Voice of the Second Circle**, ageless, marble-smooth,
white hair to his waist, waiting alone under a **crystal dome** in a warm three-storey library with the
tea already poured and **no guards in the room**. He greets all six by name and asks not about the
relic or the gate but about **home** — how many kingdoms hold the western coast, whether Valon expanded
past the river, who is king of Gidia, and whether the faithful churches still hold sway. The party,
answering honestly, describe a world of forty-five-year lifespans, toil and hunger where nobody in
power worships anything, and watch him receive it as **"Alambor the godless."** He declares them **not
prisoners** and grants free rein of the city — libraries, scholars, markets, even passage to another
Ziven city — on one condition: **Vaelor and his team accompany them as "unofficially, observers."** Then
the drop. **"The great Ilharess himself will be made aware of your arrival, and would love to meet with
you"** — and, pressed, he confirms it: **Ilharess is one of the seven members of the Hive**, the city is
his, and *"perhaps one or two other members might be interested in speaking to you as well."* On the
walk out the party works out what they have: a people who are **"a thousand percent"** trying to get
home and want *justice* against a world where nobody is left to punish, a High Examiner who is far too
warm, and — the one piece of good news — **no sign that Adune has ever spoken to them.** Jon closes by
having them declare next session's plan: the library, an escape route, and the God Isles.
