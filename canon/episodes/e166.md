# E166 — The Silver Promise

## Summary
A talking episode, and one of the biggest lore deliveries of the campaign — not a single die was rolled in anger. It opens exactly where E165 left off: six adventurers drifting weightless in the **Astral Sea**, the gate behind them sealed, the **orb of Felnriel** left bouncing on a deck in another plane, and a floating city filling the horizon. Jon's first ruling was a gut-punch — the Bag of Holding's contents, including the party's entire platinum hoard, were **destroyed, not scattered**. **Torgoth** made his third death save and woke at **1 HP**. While the party argued over a cover story — settling on *aspiring members of the Hive*, adventurers who found the Vault of Izdar, learned of the atrocities done to the Ziven dynasty, and had been hunting them ever since — **Noctis** heard creaking timber and rattling chains with no wind behind them. An impossible ship came streaking out of the void: a long narrow hull of dark violet-black wood veined with silver, 200-foot masts hung with sheets of translucent pearl instead of canvas, a bronze dragon at the prow with a pale-blue crystal in its jaws, and war-trophies swaying in the rigging. The party sensibly decided to let it pass. It harpooned them anyway — a shimmering arcane net that never quite touched them — and reeled all six aboard. Their captors were tall — seven to eight feet, though not giants — broad, green-skinned and amber-eyed, spoke no common, took every possession but their clothes, clasped **magical silver bracers** on wrists and ankles, and then… treated them impeccably: a clean white-metal cell with sleeping mats, hot water and towels brought by a healer in white robes, and word from the captain — *"you answer questions after you've slept."* In that cell **Berrian**, half-joking that this couldn't be his heaven, quietly spoke **Melora's** name — and felt wind through a tree in the back of his skull and a whisper: **"Berrian. Welcome."** After a full long rest (no hunger, no thirst, no dreams) **First Mate Xeran Vaal** — six-seven, ochre-skinned, a polished sapphire crystal where his left eye should be, silver geometric tattoos down his neck — escorted them to the stern observation chamber and **Captain Kael'vorr of the Silver Promise**. Kael'vorr poured tea into seven saucers "more for appearance than anything else," announced that *"the sea belongs to no empire — however, those who survive it owe one another a measure of honesty,"* and then, over roughly ninety minutes, laid out the entire cosmology of the Astral Sea to a party who had been in it for one day. He is **Githyanki**, native to the plane, from the city of **Tu'narath**. There have been three ages: the first, when the Astral Sea was the gods' in-between place — the bin they threw ideas they didn't like; the second, **the age of sleeping gods**, when the Sundering cast destroyed gods here and their divine essence congealed into **the God Isles** (Corellon's forests, Bahamut's crystal mountains, Ioun's infinite libraries, Tiamat's volcanic realms) — gods who *"speak to people… some, when called to join them, will hear it in the back of their mind and go there"*; and the third, **the age of the Zivens**, who were ripped out of **Maena** by the gods and dumped here **3,000 astral cycles ago**. The Zivens, Kael'vorr said, are more powerful than the Githyanki or the Slaad or nearly anything else here — and they **turned inward** rather than conquering: observatories, beacons, catalogued god isles, and above all an obsession with **color pools**, which the Zivens themselves call **fractured gates** — unstable 20-foot tears that open for minutes or hours, cluster near God Isles, and lead to Alambor, the hells, the celestial realms. *"Yes, they do intend to return. They have spent 3,000 years attempting it and have not been successful."* Pressed on whether the Githyanki want the Zivens gone, Kael'vorr was immovably neutral: *"To remove them from this place is really not within the purview of the Githyanki. They are simply part of this astral sea."* The party — genuinely rattled, half-convinced the Zivens might not be villains at all — asked to be taken to the city. Kael'vorr agreed on the strength of a single principle he repeated twice: **sailors adrift must help sailors adrift.** In exchange **Quinton** played the crew a ballad (a 21 on performance; Jon coloured it as a Gidian song of **the Gilda** — the people who *fought* the Zivens and went on to found Kierkenwall), gear was returned, and the Silver Promise swung about, dropped them close, and sailed off with Kael'vorr waving from the helm. The party then floated down into **Ilharess** — **the Gate of Worlds** — a seven-mile disc of a city under a dormant **World Gate** spire more than a mile tall, with a port, a garden quarter, a grand marketplace and over a quarter of a million inhabitants, **all drow**, all wearing the crescent-moon-and-seven-stars crest on purple. Vane led the way, deliberately nonchalant, **towing Noctis on a leash**. They were spotted on the way down. The session ends 30 yards off the ground with **thirty to forty fully-helmeted guards in angular purple-black plate**, led by a commander on horseback, marching up to meet them.

- **Movement by mind confirmed**: 3 × Intelligence score in feet, and the party is riding a slow astral current generally toward the city. The city is **three to five times the size of Valon**.
- **The cover story** was hammered out in the drift and rehearsed twice: *aspiring members of the Hive* — adventurers who found the **Vault of Izdar**, learned of the atrocities unjustly done to the Ziven dynasty, and have searched the hells, the Shadowfell and the material plane ever since, collecting artifacts that "do-gooders" kept stealing from them. **Withers** was earmarked as a corroborating name. Jon, out of character, noted the ambition of it: *"There's only seven of them in the entire Ziven dynasty. That's above Morgenrath."*
- **Noctis heard the ship first** — a DM name-callout after Vane's 17 passive lost out: creaking timber, canvas snapping with no wind, and faint chains, then a hull sliding overhead.
- **The astral ship**: long narrow hull of dark violet-black wood veined with silver; ~200-ft masts carrying stretched sheets of translucent pearl rather than cloth; a bronze dragon on the prow with an enormous pale-blue crystal in its jaws; bones, blades and charms hung in the rigging; hatches low on the hull that look built to launch smaller vessels. The party judged it uncatchable and chose to let it pass.
- **Netted:** two crew aimed a harpoon-like device; a soundless pulse, six splitting projectiles, and a shimmering arcane net that **repelled rather than touched** them — a bubble that reeled all six in. Nobody resisted. *"We don't fight it."*
- **Taken and shackled**: weapons and everything but clothing confiscated; cold silver-grey bracers clasped on arms (and legs), joined by a shimmering silver tether that grows and shrinks with movement. Limbs move freely. **Berrian's Divine Sense picked up nothing** — no celestials, fiends or undead aboard.
- **Courteous captors**: crew bowed and stepped aside as the party was walked three decks down; the brig cells are spacious, clean, polished white metal, with sleeping mats; a healer in white robes brought hot water and clean towels. The guard who had spoken two words to Noctis delivered the captain's message — *"Captain says you answer questions after you've slept."*
- **No bodily needs in the Astral Sea**: no hunger, thirst, or need for a bathroom, despite a jungle march and a sea battle. Exhaustion still applies. **Gravity works aboard the ship** — the party walks normally on deck instead of drifting.
- **Melora answered Berrian.** Speaking her name quietly, half in despair (Jon, review: Berrian does not shout it), Berrian felt *"something in the back of your head, like the wind through a tree,"* and heard **"Berrian. Welcome."** Nothing more. Later in the session Kael'vorr's lore made this land: dormant gods on the God Isles *call to people in the back of their mind*. Berrian floated finding her as a live option.
- **A full long rest** — dreamless, "the feeling of floating through space and time." On waking, Quinton's **Inspiring Leader** speech gave all six **19 temporary HP**.
- **First Mate Xeran Vaal**: ~6'7", weathered ochre skin, close-cropped black hair with a silver streak, **left eye replaced by a polished glowing sapphire crystal**, silver geometric tattoos down that side of his face and neck, leather tunic under a cloak, long silver cutlass. Vane flagged the sapphire eye as an echo of the Red Mist's captain; Jon confirmed **no connection** — different species, no resemblance.
- **Captain Kael'vorr of the Silver Promise**: tall, lean, yellow-ochre skin weathered by time rather than weather, long scarred angular face, calm assessing amber eyes, white hair braided tight, dark blue cloak over silver mail with a violet officer's cloak, a large silver greatsword, and a shimmering **compass-like medallion** at his throat. Opened with a bow and *"The sea belongs to no empire. However, those who survive it owe one another a measure of honesty. Please, friends — sit."*
- **The Githyanki are native to the Astral Sea** and were here before the gods that now dwell here. They know common only because of arrivals from other worlds. The **Silver Promise** is a merchant *and* military vessel with a crew of a hundred or more — traders, investigators, cartographers who hunt monsters and keep the peace between Githyanki cities. Home city: **Tu'narath**.
- **The three ages** (Kael'vorr's history lesson):
  - **First age** — the Astral Sea as the gods' *in-between place*: "when the gods had an idea that they weren't fond of… they tossed it, got rid of it, threw it out." Githyanki origin unknown even to Githyanki.
  - **Second age, the age of sleeping gods** — the **Sundering**. *"Gods cannot die. They can go dormant. They can be destroyed, but not eradicated."* Destroyed gods cast here left divine essence that congealed into **the God Isles**, each becoming the physical reflection of its god: **Corellon's** forests, crystal mountains where **Bahamut** rests, infinite libraries where **Ioun's** thoughts lingered, volcanic realms of **Tiamat**. Dragons, celestials, dream creatures and construct monsters emerged from the isles. Some gods speak to mortals; some call them to come.
  - **Third age, the age of the Zivens** — the Ziven cities appeared **3,000 astral cycles ago**, ripped from **Maena** by the gods themselves.
- **The Slaad** — Githyanki mortal enemies for thousands of years; demonic creations of **Tharizdun**, who dwells "deep, deep, deep into the cosmos."
- **The Zivens, per the Githyanki**: much more powerful than the Githyanki, the Slaad, or nearly anything else in the Astral Sea. After early clashes they **turned inward** — *"They weren't interested in dominating or conquering. They focused on seeking."* Observatories, magical beacons, catalogued god isles, and above all **color pools**. Kael'vorr's verdict on them is studiously neutral: not allies, not parasites — **"recent residents"** who changed the ecosystem in ways the Githyanki are still evaluating. They trade and talk occasionally and stay out of Githyanki conflicts.
- **Color pools / fractured gates** — the session's most actionable lore. Githyanki call them **color pools**; the Zivens call them **fractured gates**. They are tiny unstable **tears left behind when the gods made the place**, purposely or not; they open infrequently, are **rarely more than 20 feet across**, last **minutes to hours**, and can collapse without warning. They move constantly and appear **more often near God Isles**. Githyanki track likely locations by **astral magnetism** — which is why Kael'vorr was surprised to find the party where he did: nothing indicated a pool there. **Several known pools lead to Alambor**, but they sit in hard-to-reach places and are unreliable. Passage through is a **"violent transition"**; no Githyanki has ever attempted it, and the Zivens who tried were never seen again.
- **Seven Ziven cities**, all of similar size and all far larger than Tu'narath. The one before them is **Ilharess**, *"also known as the Gate of Worlds — or it was, at some point, known as that."* Jon confirmed in review that **the Gate of Worlds is an old name**, not a current one.
- **The Zivens want to go home.** *"Yes, they do intend to return. They have spent 3,000 years attempting it and have not been successful."* Kael'vorr considers it futile and leaves them to it.
- **The Githyanki will not take sides.** *"To remove them from this place is really not within the purview of the Githyanki… They are simply part of this astral sea."* His broader point: Alamborians are bound by time and consequence; in the Astral Sea, **things just are**.
- **Timeline peg**: it is the **500th year of this age** in Alambor (DM-confirmed), against 3,000 astral cycles since the Zivens arrived. The party's own 6:1 time-ratio arithmetic off that is **player speculation, not a DM ruling (?)**.
- **The party came clean-ish** — admitting a powerful entity back home is close to bringing the Zivens back, and that they came to judge whether that's a catastrophe. Kael'vorr: *"So you are neutral on their return. Is that what I'm hearing?"* They said yes.
- **The bargain**: Kael'vorr honours *"sailors adrift on the astral sea must always help and be gracious to sailors that they find adrift"* and offers to release them toward the city — or, alternatively, to take them to a color pool home, which "would be a little bit more of an adventure and would probably require some give and take between us. Traveling to a color pool is no small feat, and it has been a minute since I've been on a good adventure." The party chose the city.
- **Quinton sang for the crew.** Gear was fetched, a whistle assembled a hundred-plus Githyanki on the main deck, and Kael'vorr introduced him by name. Quinton played (**performance 21** — "it's good, it's not Quinton's best, but it's good"), and Jon coloured the choice: a Gidian ballad of **the Gilda — the people who came before and who fought the Zivens**, who found the waters where **Kierkenwall** now stands and built a city there. Kael'vorr: *"The Gilda. Well, it's a very inspiring tale. We shall record that in our history books."*
- **Farewell.** Gear returned, ship swung about, party stepped off into the void. The pull of the ship fades gradually as they detach. Kael'vorr watched from the helm, raised a hand, turned away, and the Silver Promise caught an astral current and was gone. *"I hope that we meet again someday, but it is very unlikely."*
- **Ilharess from above**: a circle with outcroppings, **seven miles in diameter**; a dormant **World Gate** at the centre rising **over a mile** into the void; districts including a port, a garden quarter (trees, rivers, "a whole reconstruction of nature") and a grand marketplace; **over a quarter of a million** inhabitants, though it reads as perfectly populated rather than crowded. All the inhabitants are **drow** — dark purplish skin, pointed ears, clean tunics in dark blue, white and purple, all bearing to some degree the **Ziven Crest: the crescent moon with seven stars on a field of purple-blue.**
- **The approach.** Jon described the pull as gravity-like — a tractor beam that strengthens with the mass of the nexus you fall toward. **Vane led**, acting deliberately as though he belonged; **Noctis was towed behind on a leash** (Vane: "I'm dragging him along like the dog in *Vacation*"). They were spotted well before landing — people looked up and pointed.
- **Cliffhanger**: 30–40 yards from the street, **thirty to forty guards in angular purple-black plate mail**, fully helmeted, carrying weapons the party has never seen the like of, march up led by **a commander on horseback**. End of session.
