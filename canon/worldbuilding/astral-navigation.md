# Astral Navigation

The Ziven curriculum for moving through the **Endless Between** without a ship — and the rules
for sailing one. This is the most immediately *usable* section of the library research: it is
effectively a skill manual the party can act on.

> **Source.** Jon's between-sessions research handout (17 Aug 2026), section 6. Two texts: the
> introductory manual and the advanced navigator's study.

## The four principles

***The Unmoored Traveler: Principles of Personal Navigation in the Endless Between*, by Master
Navigator Caelith Orryn**, Ilharess Collegium of Astral Transit, 73rd Revised Edition. The
standard first book.

It opens with the two assumptions that kill people:

> *The first error made by every terrestrial traveler is the assumption that the Astral Sea is
> empty. The second is the assumption that because it appears empty, direction has ceased to
> exist.*

### 1. Thought precedes motion

Swimming, kicking and climbing do nothing. They *appear* to work only because the traveller
expects them to, and **expectation itself creates the result.**

> *The limbs have accomplished nothing. The mind has.*

The method: pick a **visible** destination — a ship, a stone, a companion, a tower. Fix its
position in the mind. Then —

> *…imagine not the act of traveling toward it, but **the state of having become nearer to it**.*
>
> *The novice thinks: "I am moving toward the vessel." The practiced traveler thinks: **"The
> distance between the vessel and myself is diminishing."** The master eventually requires neither
> phrase.*

Never think merely *forward* — *"forward is a relationship between yourself and an orientation
that the Astral Sea does not recognize."*

**Stopping takes the same discipline.** Do not imagine resistance; there is none. Simply **cease
intending** to become nearer. The classic novice injury is arriving at an object while still
mentally committed to reaching it —

> *Walls remain capable of injuring you simply because the universe has become strange.*

Practise at fifty paces. Accelerate slowly. Learn deceleration before speed.

### 2. Orientation is chosen

There is no universal up. Two travellers may approach each other each believing the other is
upside down, and **neither is wrong.**

Before moving as a group, establish a **Shared Plane**: pick one visual reference (a ship's deck
is best) and have every traveller consciously accept a common orientation relative to it.

> *The vessel has not created gravity. The crew has accepted orientation.*
>
> *A group whose members independently redefine orientation during danger rapidly becomes
> disorganized.*

### 3. Distance cannot be eyeballed

No haze, no horizon, no familiar scale, no reliable celestial bodies. **An object that looks a
mile off may be hundreds of miles away — and the reverse.** Measure relative movement against
fixed references. Experienced navigators fix position from **Astral Currents, known God-Isles,
established beacons, and the signatures of major settlements.**

### 4. Know yourself — the Anchor

The most important principle, because thought governs movement:

> *Fear may create retreat before the traveler consciously chooses it. Panic may arrest movement
> entirely. An obsessive desire to reach a destination may cause acceleration beyond the
> traveler's ability to control. Confusion may alter orientation.*
>
> *And during psychic disturbances, **thoughts not originating within the traveler may become
> indistinguishable from those that do**.*

So every navigator develops an **Anchor** — *"a thought known absolutely to belong to oneself."*
A mathematical sequence, a childhood memory, a poem, a prayer, a face, a melody. It must be
familiar enough to reconstruct without effort and **personal enough that external psychic
influence cannot easily imitate its emotional texture.**

> *When uncertain of your thoughts, return to the Anchor. When uncertain of your direction, return
> to the Anchor. **When something in the darkness speaks using your own voice, return to the
> Anchor.***

Then re-establish three things: ***Where am I? Where do I intend to be? Who is doing the
intending?***

> *Lose the first and you are disoriented. Lose the second and you are adrift. Lose the third…
> and navigation is no longer your greatest concern.*

The oldest instruction given to travellers leaving Ilharess:

> ***Know where you are. Know where you intend to be. Know who you are. Lose any one of these, and
> the Sea will decide the others for you.***

> **This explains something the party already observed.** Aboard the *[Silver Promise](#)*, the
> [Githyanki](#)'s extraordinary emotional control is not merely cultural — **calmness has
> enormous practical value in a realm where thought influences movement.**

## Sailing it — currents, winds and storms

***Currents Without Water: A Navigator's Study of Astral Passage*, by Navigator-Professor Vhaelis
T'sarran**, Observatory of Astral Cartography, Ilharess. Assumes you already have the above.

**Astral Currents** are the silver streams. They are **not substances** —

> *They are directional tendencies within planar space. A useful analogy is a road upon which
> distance itself has become easier to cross. A traveler entering such a current does not
> necessarily move faster. Rather, **less movement is required to traverse the same apparent
> distance**.*

Which changes the question you ask:

> *The competent navigator does not ask "Which destination is closest?" The competent navigator
> asks: **"Which destination is most connected to where I presently am?"***

You may resist a current (rarely efficient), cross it partially (needs experience), or **align**
with it by harmonizing your intended destination with its directional tendency.

**Aboard a ship this is a collective act.** The navigator sets destination, the helmsman sets
orientation, the crew maintains coordinated intention, and **Dreamsilk sails** amplify and
stabilize that collective intention into a single navigational impulse.

> *Twenty minds imagining twenty destinations do not create greater propulsion. They create
> disagreement.*
>
> *The function of command aboard an Astral vessel is therefore partially navigational. **When the
> captain gives an order, obedience literally improves the vessel's performance.***

**Astral Winds** are *events*, not persistent features — regions of planar space abruptly
acquiring directional momentum. Causes disputed; some correlate with movement among the God-Isles,
some precede Psychic Storms, some have no detectable cause. Three options: travel with it, cross
it obliquely, or resist it —

> *The third option is generally chosen only by captains attempting to demonstrate courage to
> survivors who will later describe them as fools.*

Treat a Wind as **temporary geography**: if it carries you the wrong way, find a Current
intersecting its eventual path and let the Wind do the first leg.

> *On land, the shortest route is usually desirable. Within the Endless Between, **the fastest
> route may initially travel in entirely the wrong direction**.*

**Psychic Storms.** Never enter one voluntarily.

> *A Psychic Storm is not merely violent planar weather. **It is contamination of perception.***

Reported inside severe storms: landmarks that do not exist, vessels belonging to dead captains,
voices of absent companions, **false Astral Currents, apparent Color Pools**, and entire
settlements later proven imaginary.

> *The inexperienced navigator attempts to determine which perceptions are real. This is a
> mistake. **During a severe storm, assume none are.***

Procedure: record heading, current and intended duration **before** entry; establish an **Anchor**
for every essential crew member; ignore anything the storm offers unless independent instruments
confirm it. And —

> *Particular caution should be exercised regarding familiar voices. The Astral Sea does not
> require knowledge of your memories to deceive you. It requires only that your own mind supply
> what you most expect — or most desire — to hear.*

## Charts record relationships, not positions

Because the God-Isles drift, cities move, currents migrate and Color Pools appear and vanish:

> *An Astral chart is **not a picture of where things are**. It is a record of **how things
> relate**.*
>
> *A navigator does not travel to coordinates. A navigator travels through a sequence of
> relationships.*

Proper charts record current intersections, recurring approaches, identifiable **planar
signatures**, predicted migrations and known hazards. Every great Ziven city has a signature, and
so does every God-Isle; with training they can be perceived far beyond visual range.

### Named hazards and their signatures

| Place | How it announces itself |
|---|---|
| **[The Black Spiral](#)** | *"One does not see the Black Spiral before knowing it is near. **One feels absence.**"* |
| **[The Shattered Hoard](#)** | Disturbances resembling **heat** within magical perception. |
| **[The Moonveil](#)** | Repeated **inconsistencies in memory and reflected light**. |
| **[The Endless Labyrinth](#)** | An unusual sensation of **downward orientation**, even at enormous distance. |

> *A navigator who requires sight before recognizing danger has already approached too closely.*

T'sarran closes on humility:

> *The Astral Sea cannot be mastered. It may be studied… But every navigator eventually encounters
> something absent from every chart. When that occurs, abandon pride before abandoning caution.*
>
> *The Endless Between is older than our exile. It is larger than our maps. And it remains
> entirely indifferent to how much of it we believe ourselves to understand.*

## Why this matters at the table

- **The party can now move independently of a ship.** This is the practical answer to *"how do we
  get to the [God Isles](#)"* that doesn't depend on finding [Kael'vorr](#).
- **The Anchor is a defensive tool** against the thing that speaks in your own voice — relevant
  anywhere the party's minds are a target.
- **"Which destination is most connected to where I am?"** reframes route planning around
  currents, matching Ren Sark's E167 line that navigating the Sea *"depends on who you attach your
  sails to."*
- **The Black Spiral feels like absence** — and the [Crimson Scar](#the-fractured-gates), the
  longest-lasting fracture, sits inside its destabilized reality.
