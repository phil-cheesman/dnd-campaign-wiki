# The Sundering & the Gift (Arbor Alma)

The Sundering (also "the Great Calamity") was the world-reshaping cataclysm that occurred roughly 499–500 years before current play (E00, E112). In its aftermath many gods died, learnable transmutation magic vanished from the world, and the very landscape was reshaped (E33, E55). The era of play is dated from it: the campaign opens around the 499th annum after the Sundering, on the day the Sundering "rebirth" of the Arbor Alma is commemorated (E00). According to the coded Drow book *The Sundering* — grabbed and decoded by Evac — the catastrophe was set in motion when the pre-Sundering Drow **Zeeven Dynasty** (Jon's prose: "Ziven Dynasty"), the "chosen of the gods," were whispered to by the **Betrayer Gods** and defied the faithful gods (E00, E01, E19, E23, E89, E139). The phrase the party learned for it is "when the Gods left the tree" (E55). The world today calls itself **Alambor**, and the party has been hailed as the Saviors of Alambor (E01, E135). **Before the Sundering it was called Maena** — Jon confirmed this is in-world, not an out-of-world cartography note: *"Maena is the old name for pre-sundering Alambor"* (14 Sep 2026). It is the name the Ziven texts use throughout (E166, and the [[the-ilharess-library|Ilharess library research]]).

The realm of nations that emerged is the Trine, kept at peace by the Archivists. The **Gift** is the Arbor Alma, the great white-barked, purple-leaved tree set in a mountain-ringed glade whose harvest became the realm's transmutation currency, distributed equally by the Archivists ever since (E00, E39, E108, E119, E139). When Berrian was brought to it, the tree said "Welcome back, Berrian" / "Welcome back" (E119, E139). The **Potentium** — a blue-white crystalline power source of effectively unlimited divine energy, not native to this plane — is tied to the Sundering: combined with transmutation it caused the catastrophe, and a fragment of it (the sentience of the arch-mage Omago) empowers Adune (E89, E102, E108, E139).

## Relationships / whereabouts
- The Arbor Alma stands in the Archivist grove within **Donum Vite** ("Gift of Life"), the Archivists' tree-grown home (E00, E139). See the Donum Vite, Texair, and Archivists dossiers.
- The realm of nations that emerged — the Gidian Empire, the Carasian Kingdom, and the Luzonovian Sovereignty — is framed in [[the-trine|The Trine]].
- The **Zeeven Dynasty** and the **Betrayer Gods** are the cataclysm's architects; Adune (a surviving drow lich) seeks the Dynasty's and the Betrayers' return (E89, E102, E139, E144).
- The cult **Aum Shai** is woven through the texair raids the Archivists investigate (E102, E132); see the Aum Shai dossier.

## From Jon's worldbuilding docs

### The pre-Sundering world and the war
Before the Sundering the world was volatile. The advanced Drow **Ziven Dynasty** (canon spelling kept as "Zeeven"; "Ziven" is Jon's spelling) was extremely wise and dominated the primitive, warring smaller peoples around them. So powerful were they that they worked directly with the gods — who then still roamed the land — using **transmutation** ("the power to shape nature") to try to build a better world. Swayed by the betrayer gods (who sought to overthrow the others and seize their power), the Dynasty was convinced to help usurp the faithful gods. The faithful gods were losing the war until three of the more powerful houses among the primitive nations — what became the Trine — allied with them. Together the gods and the future Trine defeated the Dynasty and banished it to another plane of existence.

### Visual reference (Arbor Alma and its grove)
From the Archivists doc: the tree of transmutation sits at the center of the grove, an idyllic space of healthy trees, lush fields, and flowing streams that is constantly *changing* — the closer one draws to Arbor Alma, the more the ambient transmutation energy reshapes the landscape, plants, and animals into exotic, fluid, beautiful forms (the unicorn, found nowhere else in Alambor and on the Archivist sigil, appears here). A fine purple pollen dusts everything. A perfectly circular 2-mile clearing leads up an incline to a shelf of smooth, obsidian-black otherworldly stone; the massive, ancient tree has stark white bark and gnarled limbs leaning out over the clearing, its leaves shading from white to deep purple. (The texere-processing structure beside it is documented in the Archivists / Donum Vite dossiers.)
