# The Ziven Return Debate

The Zivens are not of one mind about going home. The library's recent-politics shelf holds three
competing positions, all in open circulation, all with published rebuttals. **Nobody is burning
anybody's books.**

> **Source.** Jon's between-sessions research handout (17 Aug 2026), section 5. Four texts.

## The survey — 87% would go

***A Home We Have Never Seen: On the Meaning of Return to the Astral-Born*, by Professor Sylvara
Nethryn**, *Ilharess Journal of Civic Philosophy*, **3,011th Year of Exile**. Surveyed citizens
across **all seven Ziven cities**.

> *There is a peculiar difficulty in describing homesickness for somewhere one has never been.*
>
> *Ask a citizen of Ilharess to describe Alambor and they will tell you of **rain**. Ask one from
> **Ninathar** and they will speak of mountains that do not float. Ask a child of **Felyn'riel**
> and they may describe oceans possessing shores.*
>
> *None have seen these things. Yet all speak of them with the familiarity of inheritance.*

The question asked: ***"If safe passage to Alambor were established tomorrow, would you go?"***

| Answer | Share |
|---|---|
| Yes | **68%** |
| Eventually | **19%** |
| No | **9%** |
| Declined to answer | **4%** |

**87% would return immediately or consider it.** The desire is real. But —

> *When respondents were asked what "return" should mean, **consensus disappeared**.*
>
> *Some imagined rebuilding the ancient cities. Some imagined living peacefully beside the peoples
> now inhabiting them. Some spoke of recovering ancestral lands. Others insisted that no modern
> people should surrender a home because our ancestors occupied it three millennia ago. **A small
> but vocal minority spoke openly of restoration of Ziven sovereignty.***
>
> *What unites these groups is not agreement about the future. It is **dissatisfaction with
> permanence**.*
>
> *The Astral Sea has become our home. Alambor remains our homeland. We have not yet decided
> whether those words may describe two different places.*

## The three positions

### Restorationist — "we possess the right to return"

***The Restoration Is Not a Conquest*, by Councilor Vaeren Xil**, *The Civic Concordance*. Widely
circulated, carefully worded, and the mainstream position.

Xil concedes a great deal. *"No serious advocate of Restoration proposes driving millions from
their homes."* Peoples now live on those lands; **their nations possess histories deserving
recognition irrespective of what existed before them.** The claim is narrower:

> *We possess the right to exist within the world of our creation. We possess the right to walk
> beneath its sky. We possess the right to establish communities upon its soil… And yes — we
> possess the right to seek **restitution** for what was taken.*
>
> *Restitution need not mean vengeance. Justice need not mean war. Restoration need not mean
> conquest.*
>
> *We should return openly. We should return together. We should announce ourselves before the
> peoples of Alambor and offer diplomacy before force.*

And then the sentence doing all the work:

> *If they welcome us, let history record that three thousand years of separation ended in wisdom.*
>
> ***If they refuse even our right to return… then the moral character of the dispute will have
> changed.***

It never advocates war. It arranges matters so that **if Alambor refuses, whatever follows is
Alambor's fault.**

### Astralist — "what if I am already home?"

***Must Every Road Lead Backward?*, by Scholar Thalien Orivar**, *The Astral Review*, **3,008th
Year of Exile**. Controversial, with hundreds of published rebuttals, and **freely available**.

> *I was born in Ilharess. My mother was born in Ilharess. Her mother was born in Ilharess. No
> ancestor whose name I can remember ever walked upon Alambor.*
>
> *Yet from childhood I was taught that I am waiting to go home. I ask a question for which I have
> been called ungrateful: **What if I am already home?***
>
> *Ilharess is not a prison camp. **Felyn'riel** is not a ruin. **Ninathar** is not temporary
> shelter. **Omagor** is not an encampment awaiting evacuation.*
>
> *We have constructed wonders here. We have charted the Endless Between… **We have walked upon
> the remains of gods.** We have survived.*
>
> ***At what point does survival cease to be waiting and become living?***

Orivar isn't against reopening passage — *"I would walk there myself. I would stand beneath rain…
I would visit **Phemathis**, if anything remains."* The objection is to the framing:

> *The Ziven civilization that departed Maena no longer exists. Neither does the Maena it
> departed. Alambor has changed. We have changed. **Perhaps return should mean meeting what both
> have become.** Not forcing either to become what it was.*

The line that caused the trouble:

> *There is danger in loving the past so deeply that **the living become custodians of the dead**.*

### Reconciliationist — "carry a question, not a claim"

***The First Conversation*, by Ambassador Serelis Vael**, Institute for Material Reconciliation.
**The smallest faction — and potentially the party's best ally in the city.**

Vael's thesis: the danger isn't the Zivens, it's **three thousand years of incompatible histories
meeting one another.**

> *Perhaps we are remembered as tyrants. Perhaps as servants of Betrayer Gods. Perhaps as
> destroyers of Maena. Perhaps we are scarcely remembered at all.*
>
> *Now imagine the sky opening above one of their cities and a civilization they believed dead
> emerging through it. **We may call this Homecoming. They may call it invasion.** Neither word
> changes what occurs. Only understanding can.*
>
> *Therefore the first Ziven to return should not carry a spear. Nor should they carry a claim.
> **They should carry a question: "What do you remember of us?"** And then they should listen.*
>
> *Reconciliation requires something far more difficult than defeating an enemy. It requires
> allowing another person to describe the harm they believe you caused **without immediately
> explaining why they are mistaken**.*
>
> *If we cannot accomplish this, then we are not prepared to return. We are merely prepared to
> arrive.*

But reconciliation is **not** acceptance of permanent exile:

> *Diplomacy must not become another word for waiting. Our exile cannot be inherited without end…*
>
> *But if, after all of this, we are told that no Ziven may ever again walk upon the world that
> gave birth to our people, then we will have learned something important.*
>
> *The first conversation must occur. **What follows depends upon the answer.***

## Why this matters at the table

- **The party is already having the first conversation.** High Examiner Aethon Valise spent E167
  interviewing them about Alambor's kingdoms, monarchs and religion — *"What do you remember of
  us?"* in all but name, and he called the world they described **"Alambor the godless."**
- **Every position ends at the same door.** Restorationist, Astralist and Reconciliationist all
  accept that exile should end; they disagree only on terms. Nobody argues for staying put
  forever — which makes the dormant [World Gate](#) the one thing they all need.
- **Serelis Vael and the Institute for Material Reconciliation are a findable faction** with a
  stated interest in exactly what the party is: material-plane witnesses who can be asked, rather
  than conquered.
- **Dissent is permitted**, which tells the party something about how Ilharess governs. Jon's E167
  framing was *"It's too perfect. It's almost odd."* The Zivens don't need to ban Orivar; **they
  believe they can win the argument.**

## Dating note

The handout dates works to the **"3,011th Year of Exile"** and **"3,008th Year of Exile,"** and
the Ilharess architectural history to **"2,847 AS."**

**These are two different counts** — Jon ruled on it, 14 Sep 2026, verbatim:

> *Ante-Sundering is linked to but not the same timeline as the year of exile since time in the
> astral sea is measured differently than alambor, but in both cases they are post the sundering
> incident. the timelines just may not line up completely.*

So: **AS** (Jon's label: *Ante-Sundering*) and the **Year of Exile** are **linked but distinct**
era counts, **both reckoned after the Sundering**, and they do **not** necessarily align, because
astral time is measured differently from Alambor time. A date in one **cannot be assumed
convertible** into the other. (Jon's label *Ante-Sundering* sits oddly with his own statement that both counts are
post-Sundering; the tension is unexplained **(?)**.)

The **Year of Exile** count still sits comfortably beside Kael'vorr's **3,000 astral cycles**
since the Zivens arrived (E166) — both look to be reckoned astral-side **(?)**. What the ruling
forbids is treating **2,847 AS** as a point on that same line. The party's 6:1 astral-to-Alambor time ratio
remains player speculation, not a ruling.

## Open questions

- **Which faction does the [Hive](#) belong to** — or does it sit above the argument? Every public
  text here is civil. The [Vessels](#vessels-of-the-seven) catalogue has a Hive classification
  authority sealing records.
- The other **three Ziven cities** are never named — in the handout or anywhere else. Four are:
  **Ilharess, Ninathar, Felyn'riel, Omagor**, all archmage names. Asked whether the remaining
  three were therefore *Izzdar*, *Zarryne* and *Valonim*, Jon ruled the idea **"too neat"**
  (14 Sep 2026). **The three are simply unnamed.**
- Where does **[Adune](#)** fit? He wants the Zivens back and hates *all* the gods. None of these
  three positions is his.
