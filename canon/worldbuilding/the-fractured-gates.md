# The Fractured Gates

The Ziven name for what the party has been calling **color pools** — the exits from the
[Astral Sea](#). **DM-confirmed (Jon, E167 review):** color pools are *"rifts between planes here
in the Astral sea"* — distinct from **divine gates** (which are *"where the god isles are"*) and
from the **World Gate** (which *"was part of Ilharess in Maena"*). See
[the gate taxonomy](#astral-sea). This page is the Ziven **scholarly** account of them, and it is the direct
answer to the party's sharpest open question out of E167: *why can't the Zivens just leave?*

> **Source.** Jon's between-sessions research handout (delivered to the table 17 Aug 2026),
> answering the library research declared at the end of E167. Three academic papers, quoted
> verbatim below.

## The numbers

> *Of the **seven hundred and twelve** documented interplanar fractures observed during the last
> eleven centuries, only **three** have demonstrated recurrence sufficient to merit formal
> classification.*
>
> — Professor **Vaelis Orun**, *On the Stability Thresholds of Interplanar Fractures*,
> Collegium of Spatial Mechanics, Ilharess

**712 fractures documented. 3 stable enough to name.**

Orun's core distinction:

> *A gate is a controlled continuity between two locations. These phenomena are discontinuities
> temporarily mistaken for continuity.*
>
> *The distinction is not semantic.*

A conventional portal has a stable origin, a stable terminus, a measurable duration, and an
arcane structure that can sustain passage. **A Fractured Gate has none of these with acceptable
consistency.**

## The three named fractures

| Name | Where / behaviour | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **The Emerald Tear** | Appears within the **Verdant Current**; observed at least **17 times** in the period of reliable observation. | *"Its precise location has never repeated."* The surrounding landmass itself migrates, and **anchored beacons have failed** because the local geography shifts faster than fixed coordinates stay useful. |
| **The Sapphire Mirror** | The inverse problem — the aperture is *"comparatively gentle."* | **The destination cannot be predicted.** *"In no recorded appearance has a terminus been observed twice."* **Emotional resonance appears to influence destination**, but attempts to quantify it produced contradictory results. |
| **The Crimson Scar** | The **greatest planar coherence** and the **highest mortality risk**; sustains transit longer than either of the others. | It exists **inside the destabilized reality surrounding the [Black Spiral](#)**, where *"distance, chronology, and identity are themselves unreliable variables."* |

Orun's conclusion, and the official public Ziven position:

> *In short: the problem is not that passage appears impossible. The problem is that passage
> cannot be demonstrated to be survivable, repeatable, or controllable.*
>
> *A single traveler stepping through one of these phenomena may be considered an experiment. A
> civilization attempting the same is negligence.*
>
> *Until a traveler can depart, arrive, report, and reproduce the journey under observable
> conditions, claims of successful planar return must remain outside the boundaries of accepted
> scholarship.*

**No confirmed successful crossing exists** — not because the Zivens think it impossible, but
because **nobody has crossed and come back with proof.**

## Why individuals leaving doesn't solve anything

**Magister Thalara Venn**, *Transit Capacity and Population Preservation in Unstable Planar
Apertures*, Directorate of Civil Continuity. This is a **logistics** paper, not a magic one, and
it answers the party's question head-on.

Venn grants every generous assumption — a fifteen-foot aperture, a stable duration of one hour,
and no concerns at all about destination, structural collapse, local guardians, or whether
travellers survive transit — and still says the throughput is negligible against the population
of a Ziven city.

> *The question is therefore commonly posed incorrectly.*
>
> *It is not: "Can a Ziven pass through?" It is: **"Can the Ziven people pass through?"** These
> are not equivalent propositions.*
>
> *Any solution that restores one hundred citizens while abandoning one hundred thousand cannot
> properly be described as restoration. It is triage.*
>
> *Any solution that restores scholars, officers, or members of privileged houses while leaving
> the general population behind cannot properly be described as liberation. It is desertion
> dressed in arcane terminology.*

Then the political half — and this is the part that should make the party sit up:

> *A handful of unidentified Ziven citizens appearing unpredictably across the Material Plane
> would possess no recognized legal status, no secured territory, no diplomatic protection, and
> no means of explaining their arrival…*
>
> ***One traveler may be mistaken for an anomaly. Ten may be interpreted as infiltration. A
> hundred may be interpreted as preparation for war.***
>
> *The very method by which we attempted to return could therefore manufacture the conflict we
> seek to avoid.*
>
> *If there is to be a homecoming, it must be visible. It must be deliberate. It must be
> undeniable.*

The closing line is famous in Restorationist circles:

> ***We did not survive three thousand years together so that we might return home separately.***

Noble — and **extremely convenient for the [Hive](#)**, because it rules out every small-scale
option and leaves only a single large-scale return through a working gate. Which is what the
[World Gate](#) is for, and what [Adune](#) has been assembling relics to power.

## Boundary memory — why repeated use is worse, not better

**Doctor Sereth Cael**, *Planar Signature Accumulation During Repeated Fracture Transit*,
Observatory of Boundary Phenomena. The most operationally dangerous idea in the whole handout.

Every crossing strains the boundary it passes through. One traveller usually leaves no trace.
Repeated transit does not:

> *Each passage leaves residual alignment between origin and destination. If multiple travelers
> traverse the same fracture within a sufficiently narrow period, those alignments begin to
> reinforce one another.*
>
> *This phenomenon, herein termed **boundary memory**, may temporarily **increase aperture
> stability**. It also **increases detectability**.*
>
> *A sufficiently large series of crossings would create an energetic signature visible not
> merely to Ziven instrumentation but to any entity, order, or intelligence capable of monitoring
> planar boundaries.*
>
> ***The possibility that such observers include servants of the Faithful Gods must be considered
> probable rather than speculative.***

The paradox, stated plainly:

> *The more successful the crossing becomes, the more visible it becomes. The more visible it
> becomes, the less likely it is to remain viable.*

And the institutional position that follows from it:

> *For this reason, the Directorate continues to reject clandestine migration proposals.*
>
> *Our objective is not to breach the boundary unnoticed. Our objective is to restore lawful
> continuity between the Astral and Material worlds… We do not seek to invade a foreign land. We
> seek recognition of our right to return to the land from which our civilization originated.*
>
> *A permanent passage must therefore satisfy three conditions: **stability, capacity, and
> legitimacy**. The Fractured Gates satisfy none.*

The paper's last line is the whole page in nine words:

> ***A fracture may save a traveler. It cannot restore a people.***

## Why this matters at the table

- **E167's biggest open question is answered.** The Zivens *can* send individuals through. They
  refuse to, on logistical, diplomatic and doctrinal grounds — and, per Cael, because doing it
  repeatedly would light them up for whoever is watching the boundaries.
- **The party is a walking counter-example.** They arrived by rupturing a Bag of Holding inside a
  cauldron (E165), and Ilharess's citizens guessed at *"another fractured gate"* on sight (E167).
  Under Orun's standard they are the *"traveler who departed, arrived, and can report."* They
  have not yet reproduced the journey.
- **The Crimson Scar sits inside the Black Spiral's destabilized reality** — the one fracture
  that sustains transit longest is in the worst possible place, and the Black Spiral is a
  **[God-Isle](#)** (Jon, 14 Sep 2026). If the party wants a way home that isn't the World Gate,
  this is the shortlist, and it's a bad shortlist.
- **Emotional resonance steering the Sapphire Mirror** is a hook with the party's name on it.

## Open questions

- Where are the three named fractures *now*? Orun says positions never repeat; the
  [charts](#astral-navigation) record **relationships, not coordinates**.
- Does the Ziven Directorate know the party's arrival was a fracture event — and does that make
  them evidence, or a problem to be classified? (?)
- **Boundary memory cuts both ways:** does the party's own crossing already carry a signature
  something could follow back? (?)
- Who or what actually monitors planar boundaries? Cael calls servants of the Faithful Gods
  *"probable rather than speculative"* but names nobody.
