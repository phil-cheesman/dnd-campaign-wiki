# House Tifar

The pre-Sundering Ziven house of **Morgenrath** — Councilor of Arcane Law, jurist, diplomat, and
the man the party knows three thousand years later as **[Adune](#)**.

The party went looking for his name in the Ilharess library and found, instead of a monster, **a
husband and father who argued against the war and lost everything.**

> **Source.** Jon's between-sessions research handout (17 Aug 2026), section 4 — *"Are there any
> references to Morgenrath?"* Three works: his own legal treatise, the family's private
> correspondence, and a historical reconstruction of his last years.

> **Naming — settled.** This handout consistently says **Morgenrath Tifar / House Tifar**, and Jon
> confirmed it on **14 Sep 2026**: ***"Tifar is correct."*** The wiki-wide rename has been run:
> **[Adune (Morgenrath Tifar)](#)** is the canonical heading, and *Gafar* — a transcription error
> that appears only in material transcribed off the recording — is retained as an alias only.
>
> Jon had already written **Tifar** to the table once before, on **9 Oct 2025**: *"the Reveal of
> the Shadowed Moon aka **Morgenraith Tifar** aka **Adun**."*

## The man before

***Principles of Arcane Sovereignty and Responsible Practice*, by Councilor Morgenrath Tifar**,
written **31 years before the Sundering.** His most influential legal work; Ziven jurists still
cite it. This is the text that tells the party they have found **the** Morgenrath.

> *The capacity to perform an act cannot itself constitute justification for its performance.*
>
> *…among practitioners of the higher arcane disciplines we have permitted a peculiar inversion of
> this reasoning. **The more extraordinary the capability, the more readily we excuse its exercise
> as inquiry.***
>
> *We ask whether a thing can be summoned before asking whether it should be summoned… We ask
> whether boundaries may be crossed and congratulate ourselves upon crossing them before
> determining what stood upon the opposite side.*
>
> ***Discovery does not absolve the discoverer of consequence.***

Then he follows the argument all the way up:

> *Power establishes capability. Law establishes authority. Justice determines whether either has
> been properly exercised.*
>
> *If it governs the relationship between citizen and archmage, then intellectual honesty requires
> that we consider its application between peoples, between rulers and subjects, and — however
> uncomfortable the question — **between mortal and divine.***
>
> *To state that a god possesses the power to command is merely an observation. To state that the
> god therefore possesses the **right** to command is a philosophical proposition requiring its
> own defense.*
>
> *I do not write this in rejection of the gods. Quite the contrary. I have learned much beneath
> their instruction, and **from [Erathis](#) perhaps more than any living teacher**.*
>
> *A law that governs only the powerless is not law. It is permission granted by the powerful.*

Ziven jurists call the result the **Tifar Principle**:

> ***"Power does not confer authority merely because lesser power cannot resist it."***

His work also helped set Ziven standards for dangerous magical research, summoned creatures,
magical liability, and **the rights of peoples subjected to Ziven law.**

## The family

***The Private Correspondence of the House of Tifar*, compiled by Archivist Liraeth Vaun.** Mostly
estate administration and politics — but **several hundred letters between Morgenrath and his wife
Zarra survived.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Zarra** | Wife. Teased him about working too much. |
| **Minonet** | Son. Won a wager against his father. |
| **Valtel** | Son. Wrote legal arguments about the ownership of conjured property. |
| **Illrin** | Son. Practised transformation on his brother's possessions. |

Most of it is extraordinarily mundane: he complains about Council meetings, asks after his sons
while travelling, sends books home, and **repeatedly apologises for missing meals.**

> *Zarra,*
>
> *The Council has discovered another matter of such unprecedented importance that it apparently
> cannot survive until morning.*
>
> *You may inform **Minonet** that he has won our wager and that I owe him three silver crowns. I
> maintain that this victory was achieved through circumstances beyond my control and therefore
> constitutes neither skill nor prophecy.*
>
> *Tell **Valtel** that I have received his revised argument concerning the ownership of conjured
> property. It remains entirely wrong, though considerably more impressively wrong than the
> previous version. I shall explain why when I return.*
>
> *And **Illrin** is not to practice transformation upon his brother's possessions again. The fact
> that he successfully restored the boots does not improve the matter…*
>
> *I should return tomorrow before the evening meal. I know I said the same yesterday. You are
> permitted to remind me of this.*
>
> *M.*

And the letter historians cite when discussing his priorities — declining a promotion:

> *I have declined the appointment.*
>
> *Yes, I know what it represents. Yes, I understand another may not be offered.*
>
> *But the posting requires three years away from **[Phemathis](#)**, and Minonet will be nearly
> grown by the time I return.*
>
> ***There will always be another council. There will not be another childhood.***

## The fall

***The Failure of Concord: Proceedings of the Tifar Mediation*, by Historian Caelira Thess** — a
reconstruction of Morgenrath's increasingly desperate attempts to mediate between Ziven leadership
and the divided gods in the final years before the Sundering.

The arc of his reputation is three words long: **moderate → naïve → disloyal.**

His final major address before the **Council of Arcane Law**:

> *Again I am asked to declare upon which side I stand. I confess that I have grown weary of the
> question.*
>
> ***I stand upon Maena. That should be sufficient.***
>
> *Every faction has explained to me why the other must yield. **None has explained what remains
> when none do.***
>
> *We speak now of victory as though gods might conveniently wage war somewhere beyond our
> borders. Where? Upon whose fields? Above whose homes?*
>
> ***Should gods make war upon gods, there shall be no distant battlefield… Maena itself shall
> become their weapon.***
>
> *I reject the assertion that peace is cowardice simply because war requires courage. I reject
> the assertion that loyalty demands blindness. And I reject most strongly the proposition that
> one must choose between loving one's people and admitting when one's people are wrong.*
>
> *If justice exists only when it favors us, then it is not justice. If divine law exists only
> when mortals obey it, then it is not law.*

**The speech was poorly received.** His standing collapsed afterward. Even his relationship with
**Erathis**, whom he had long respected and personally known, became strained. His continued
advocacy for **the [Gilda](#) and other subject peoples** isolated him further from militant Ziven
factions.

Then the record breaks.

## The ledger

There are references to **violence around [Phemathis](#)**. Then House Tifar's records read:

> **Zarra Tifar — deceased.**
> **Minonet Tifar — deceased.**
> **Valtel Tifar — deceased.**
> **Illrin Tifar — deceased.**

**Morgenrath himself is never recorded as dead.** His Council attendance simply degrades:

| | |
|---|---|
| | *Councilor Morgenrath Tifar — **Absent: Diplomatic Assignment*** |
| ↓ | *Absent: **Phemathis Emergency*** |
| ↓ | ***Absent*** |
| ↓ | *(nothing)* |

**The Sundering follows soon afterward.**

No reliable Ziven record places him anywhere **after** it. He is not listed among the Zivens who
arrived in the Astral Sea, and there is no confirmed account of his death. Modern histories give
him one line:

> **Morgenrath Tifar — 670 PS–?**
> *Councilor of Arcane Law, jurist, scholar and diplomat.*
> ***Presumed lost during the Sundering.***

## Why this matters at the table

- **It corroborates committed canon.** `canon/npcs/adune.md` already records Adune as *"a
  surviving Zeeven whose wife and sons died in the Sundering."* The handout supplies **their
  names**, and that they died at **Phemathis**, **before** the Sundering rather than in it.
- **The party now has his voice from before.** The man who wrote *"discovery does not absolve the
  discoverer of consequence"* and *"there will not be another childhood"* is the same man who
  murdered King Felix at the war council.
- **Jon confirmed in E167 that Morgenrath blames and hates *all* the gods — Betrayers included.**
  Section 4 explains why: he argued against both sides, was called disloyal by his own people for
  it, and then lost his entire family anyway.
- **He personally knew Erathis** — god of civilization, main god of Luzonovia, and namesake of the
  Vallon temple the party has visited.
- **Ilharess's own archives do not know he survived.** If the Zivens learn otherwise, that is
  leverage the party is carrying and hasn't spent.

## Open questions

- **What happened at Phemathis?** The chapter is *"frustratingly incomplete."* The family died in
  violence there; Morgenrath was logged absent for a *"Phemathis Emergency"* and then vanished.
- **Who killed them?** The handout does not say, and neither does any committed canon.
- **The age problem.** Committed canon calls Adune a **1,200-year-old** lich; this handout has him
  born **670 PS** and lost at a Sundering the Zivens date **3,000+ years** back. E167 established
  that Alambor's **500 annum** are counted only from Arbor Alma and that the unrecorded
  **Hollow Years** sit in between — but the two figures still don't obviously reconcile. **(?)**
- **Is any of House Tifar in the Astral Sea?** Four deceased, one unrecorded. Nobody has asked
  whether the family has descendants, records, or a tomb in the [Lower Works](#ilharess).
