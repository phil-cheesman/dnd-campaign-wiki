# Gidia

British-style trade monarchy and the campaign's dominant empire, ruled by King Felix the Cold with High Chancellor Adune at his side. Its capital is Kierkenwall (briefly Goldcrest under Sofia Rose Hand); slavery is outlawed in Gidia proper while its vassal islands of Mandalonia supply manual labor. Gidia built power through vassal states and its navy, in contrast to land-power Carasia, with whom it has had good standing but a rocky past. The empire's institutions include the Knights of Gidia, the Counsel of Felix, and the texair economy. Gidia raced Luzonovia for the alien temple find in Jazacha, escalated to DEFCON 4 before the royal wedding, and requested the wedding itself while extending loans to a struggling Carasia. The party served Gidia for much of the campaign — honored as Defenders of Sutrey, Proctors of Evershire, and Slayers of Valadon, with medals granting access throughout the empire. Everything turned after Adune murdered King Felix at the war council and framed the party: Gidia, now steered by Adune and Sir Trent Hightower, launched wars of conquest — taking the entire north, the Morian Kingdom, and Dalacia, invading Carasia, and threatening Tarlif and Paratha — while the party became enemies of the state.

## From Jon's worldbuilding docs

**Founding & identity.** The Gidian Empire was founded in 17 P.T., when Alon the Wise — the great mage and first Archivist — brought the Trine together at the Council of Reckoning and forged their tenuous alliance, which has lasted some 482 annum. The Gidians were previously a people known as **The Gilda**: a hardy, scrappy folk whose whole identity was surviving the dangers that beset them on all sides, hemmed in by powerful neighboring nation-states, so they took to the sea for trade and expansion. At that first Council, **Gidian the Tall** (the empire's founder and first monarch, reigned 17–42 P.T.) represented the Gilda and negotiated their northern territories. He was later criticized — never directly to his face — for negotiating poorly, leaving Gidia hemmed in with poor land and no room to expand into adjacent territory.

**Ruling style — monarchy.** A strict noble hierarchy: King/Queen (leader of the people of Gidia) > Duke/Duchess (the ruler's sons/daughters) > Marquess/Marquise (provincial leader, resident in the provincial capital town) > Earl (village leader) > Viscount/Viscountess (vassal-state representative). The commoners are hardy; the nobles are "stuck up." The military holds a great deal of power and authority and can be brutish — town guards tend to live in their town and never venture out, while seasoned veterans have traveled far and seen (and display) the cruelty of the world. Accents are English; the cuisine and drink are somewhat bland ("think English").

**People & races.** Primarily Humans, Halflings, and Half-Elves, though all manner of peoples live there; there is no outward racism toward foreigners, but outsiders become the talk of the town and may face rude questions or gentle interrogation from locals. Common is the native tongue (a legacy of their prolific trading); the nobles sometimes still speak Old Gidian, a long-forgotten dialect.

**Religion.** Downplayed — all gods are accepted except the betrayer gods, but religious fervor is not prized; the Gidians have let worship fall to the back of their minds, putting their interests at home and abroad first. The Post-Sundering pantheon's six gods have temples in Gidia only on rare occasions (E137).

**Military.** A mixed bag — strong, hearty soldiers all around. Not as prolific in the arcane arts as Carasia, and lacking the barbarian mercenaries or the Volstrucker "Scourgers" of the Luzonovians, but more inventive in tactics and forms of combat — and capable of being quite cruel and merciless when needed. Their allies amplify them: the Maadolonian navy (said by some to be the best in Alambor) and the elite **Dalacian Cavaliers** (marksmen). Every city and town in the Empire is walled and heavily guarded, especially along the border with the Morian Kingdom of the dwarves.

**Learning.** Study is not a priority for the general population, so most Gidians are not well-versed in the arcane, scientific, or historical. The exception — and the empire's primary seat of study and learning — is the **Knights of Gidia**, an elite paladin guild that balances scholarship with warrior training at locations throughout the empire ("When enemies strike, we strike back"). Its rank ladder runs Chevall, Marcheon, Redhawk, Vindicator, Righteous Hand.

**Economy & currency.** Gidian coin is regular currency but stamped with the Gidian crest (each faction has its stamp on the coin), which makes it hard to trade or barter abroad and can draw unwanted attention. Travel through the countryside is mostly pleasant — fertile rolling hills, small lakes and rivers, a favorable summer climate for good harvests (so long as the texere is flowing), small villages between the large province-governing towns — though travelers may be harassed by road patrols or by roving bandits.

**Population & geography.** Total population ~29,000; the capital city holds ~11,000. The empire comprises three provinces:

- **Langdale** (capital province), nicknamed "Three Rivers" — rolling hills and sprawling farmland, the empire's primary food and texere center, with prosperous if humble villages and texere markets as the focal point of every town (E27). Its capital, **Kierkenwall** (Jon's prose spells it "Kierkenwall"/"Kierkewall"; canonical play-spelling kept — see Open questions), sits where three rivers meet and swell into the great river that winds into the Bay of Resplendence (E52, E67). Commerce flows up and down the rivers in huge barge trains — grain, cotton, and food going out; mountain goods, coastal spoils, and Dalacian wagons coming in. Along the northern border with Dalacia run several fort chains and communication posts to defend against raiders from the hills and mountains.
- **Wintervale** (western highlands; Jon's prose also "Wintervail") — bordering the Morian Kingdom of the dwarves; hardy "Highlanders" living in fortified hill towns on barren rocky terrain, with little farming, small mines and ironworks, and some fishing. They dislike their southern neighbors the Carasian Kingdom, skirmish constantly with mountain tribes of orcs and goblins, and are openly racist toward the Morian dwarves. The Empire invests in it as a strategic province. Its major town is **Ironforge** (~3,000), and its great export is fighting men — **The Highland Brigade**, a fierce and hardy fighting force (cf. the Iron Forge stronghold, E84, E135).
- **Strathmore** (Jon's prose province header also "Stathmore") — the coastal province bordering Archivist territory, teeming with lakes, rivers, and two long coastlines, so water-bound that locals say every man, woman, and child swims more miles than they walk and boats outnumber horses. Trade is its primary commerce; ships from across Alambor deliver goods, taxes, and royalties from the vassal states, and it docks the Empire's Navy, alongside fishing and water-vessel craftsmanship. Its major town is **Goldcrest** (~8,000) — built upon the water (Venice-like), the largest port in all of Alambor and the wealthiest city in Gidia, much to the consternation of Kierkenwall, and a favorite haunt of high officials and nobles for its wealth and entertainment (E53–E56).

**Goldcrest in detail (E54–E56).** Founded in 168 P.T. by **Eric Shipbuilder**, who — after the Carasians completed their canal — commissioned hundreds of ships and birthed Gidia as a seafaring nation; it began as a small shipbuilding town. **Rhys the Bold** later used it as a launching point to explore Gidia, the Bay of Resplendence, and the wider Trine waters, and **Nicolas the Merchant** grew its commerce immensely. After a period of disrepair, Goldcrest backed **Sophia Rosehand** (the played NPC "Sofia Rose Hand", E54, E55) in her contest for the throne; on winning she moved the capital there during her reign, and — a devout follower of Erathis — made it not only the merchant capital but the most learned and beautiful city in Gidia (some locals still call it "Our Fair Lady," after both Erathis and Sophia). This bred lasting resentment in Kierkenwall, both for the loss of the seat of power and for Goldcrest's Erathis devotion; the capital later returned to Kierkenwall, but Goldcrest folk still consider themselves superior to the rest of Gidia. Its patron god is **Erathis**. The city's four districts (E55): **Endless Falls** (District 1 — the religious/older quarter, dominated by a cliffside bastion with the statue of Rhys, an impossible never-ending waterfall, the temple of Erathis "the Lady," the statue of Nicolas the Merchant, and a row of mausoleums); **The Garrison** (District 2 — a walled military fortress split by water "locks," seat of the military leader, with the military dockyard and sheltered docks); **Hightower** (District 3 — the posh district where nobles and dignitaries live); and **Eastown** (District 4 — where the bulk of the common folk live and the three arenas, the Salt and Steel Company, and Shipyard Isle sit). Approaching from the sea, ships pass under an ornate bridge over 100 ft high (lined with shops and a defensive tower, flanked by two ballista forts) into a wide bay where three rivers flow in from north, south, and east; the cliffs are ringed with whitewashed walls bearing rose banners — a field of blue with a deep red rose at the center.

**Festivals.** *Midsommer* — the first day of the summer sextet, when days are longest; primarily a festival of Pelor, marked by dancing, eating, drinking, and praising Pelor. *Midwinter* — a less-celebrated (and in some cultures frowned-upon) holiday honoring the Raven Queen and the gods who passed in the Sundering; a journey into the wilderness in groups where great fires are lit, mind-affecting plants and herbs are taken, and the spirits of the gods are said to be seen. *Feast of Vita messis* — a floating holiday marking the fall of the texerine, signaled by the Archivist order's beacons to warn that the long winter is coming; a time of reflection, meager food and drink, prayer and gifts, and the walking and blessing of the fields (prepared by mages, a necessary act for the texere to bear fruit).

**Royal lineage (Gidian the Tall → Felix the Cold).** Jon's house tree runs (founder first): Gidian the Tall (17–42); Filip the Young (42–54), a poor, short-lived king who fell in battle against the Morian Kingdom; Declan the Old (54–103), under whom Gidia took the Free States of Dalacia as a vassal right before his death in 102 P.T.; Cassius Peacemaker (103–126), who made peace with the Morian Kingdom; Stephen the Cruel (126–166), a paranoid tyrant who tried to exterminate the elite class; **Eric Shipbuilder** (166–185), who — after the Carasian canal — commissioned hundreds of ships and birthed Gidia as a seafaring nation; **Rhys the Bold** (185–209), an adventurer who spent his reign exploring Alambor by ship; **Nicolas the Merchant** (209–241), who turned Rhys's discoveries into trade and made Gidia thrive; Lucian the Hunter (241–269), who hid in his estates and hunted; **Sophia Rosehand** (269–301), the one and only female ruler of Gidia (because Lucian fathered only daughters) — much of the Empire opposed her, but she ruled with an iron fist, crushing insurrections and coups without mercy, and moved the capital to Goldcrest; Paul the Pious (301–326); Filip Longstrider (326–352); **William the Conqueror** (352–389), who conquered the Islands of Maandolin; Edwin the Fearless (389–412); Cassius the Second (412–443), so like Cassius Peacemaker that he was dubbed thus, who strengthened ties within the Trine and with Carasia; Lachlan the Lover (443–467); Edward Strongjaw (467–477), Felix's father, a harsh, quick-tempered drinker who died at 41; and **Felix the Cold** (477–present), once thought a just and joyful man until his beautiful but fragile wife died young and unexpectedly, which turned him cold and harsh (E24, E27, E135–E136).

**Vassal states.** The **Islands of Maadolonia** — six island nation-states taken by force in 327 P.T.; a hardy seafaring people, uneasy under Gidian rule but not in open rebellion, who supply the bulk of Gidia's navy in men and ships (E00, E52, E53). The **Free States of Dalacia** — a confederacy of self-governing towns (capital Aberdeen), farmers of wheat and cotton, who became a Gidian vassal in 102 P.T. when, on the brink of starvation from the failing weather and lack of transmutation magic, they gladly submitted to preserve their way of life (E52, E67, E69). Gidia generally stays out of Dalacia's affairs, sending emissaries only when necessary.
