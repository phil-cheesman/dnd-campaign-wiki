# Carasian Kingdom

French-flavored nation ruled by Queen Beatrice "the Lion's Blood," with its capital at Vallon (the Halls of Wonder palace; early notes call the capital "Balom"). The campaign's first act began here: the party shipwrecked on the Carasian coast and made its way through Carstone and Sultray. Carasia is historically a land power producing food and goods, traditionally close to Gidia. By the wedding era it had fallen on hard times — texair flow from Alborama was poor, so it accepted Gidian loans, and Gidia requested the royal wedding of Prince Thomas to Princess Anabel. After Thomas was stabbed by his best man Justin at the wedding, Beatrice banished the party until they avenged him, blamed Luzonovia, and declared war, assembling a 1,000-ship armada against Rynestag. The Carasian–Luzonovian war produced the carnage at Bootenhill and the trap at Airhark Lake. The party later intervened and exposed Gidian interference: both nations made peace and now look to group up against Adune's Gidians. By the late war status, Vallon and Grellier were the only Carasian cities left standing. One council note claims the Carasians were "the rightful Trine overlords."

## From Jon's worldbuilding docs

**Founding and identity.** The Kingdom of Carasa was founded in 17 P.T. (Post Texerine), one of the three members of the Trine. It coalesced from a people formerly known as the **tribes of Carassis** — a blend of gypsies, adventurers, and small city-states, a loose alliance at first, united by a shared zeal for life: food, drink, creation, the arcane arts, art, and beauty, and unafraid of decadence or new experiences. They find strength in inclusivity and valuing each other's strengths. Sharing the most land borders and the most neighbors of any nation — and trading freely with all of them — they are the **wealthiest of the Trine**. At the founding **Council of Reckoning**, **Anestasia the Bold** (the Carasian Kingdom's first monarch and the sole female representative of the Trine) negotiated their eastern territories with flair and bravado; the lands she claimed were the most valuable of the Trine but also the most exposed (vast, heavily bordered, and abutting the southern Horde threat). She secured them by quickly forming alliances with neighboring states and riding at the head of her army to help win a key victory against the Horde Alliance (Gusaash). (The Council of Reckoning that bound the Trine was convened by Alon the Wise, the first Archivist; see the Archavisian Order.)

**Geography.** The land runs from a hot, arid tropical desert in the south — toward the Kingdom of Parathia and the colder, barren country around Lake Mena, where most travel in headdress and robes — northward into a verdant heartland paradise of rolling hills, woods, lakes, vineyards, breweries, and small farms with no walls around the villages, up to the mountains of the Morian Kingdom (logging, forestry, and wild pelts are major exports there). The capital province holds most of the coastline, in a Mediterranean register, along the Bay of Resplendence where it meets the Trine waters. Roads are well maintained but winding; outposts are sparse except a solid fort-and-wall chain along the Horde Alliance border. Patrols are famously friendly and rarely check papers, but the south and some inner highways are plagued by bandits.

**Ruling style — monarchy.** Succession passes to the **eldest heir regardless of gender**. The hierarchy: **High King/Queen** (leader of the Carasians) > **Prince/Princess** (sons/daughters) > **Duke/Duchess** (provincial leader, seated in the provincial capital town) > **Viscount/Viscountess** (village leader) > **Baron/Baroness** (vassal-state representative).

**Religion.** All gods are accepted equally (the betrayer gods excepted); no single god holds primacy, and shrines and temples to all stand in seemingly equal standing throughout the nation. (Festivals nonetheless attach to particular gods — see below.)

**People, races, and culture.** Accent is French. The High Elves make up the court, and elves and half-elves are the most prevalent people, but this is by far the most diverse and accepting land — the notable exception being a few factions of **elven purists** near the Parathian border who share the Parathians' pride in pure ancient blood. Nobles carry themselves with refinement and commoners with boisterousness, but all share the same passions: innate curiosity, fierce temper, a quick flame of love and lust, and hunger for new and exotic experiences. Their faults run to being too trusting (especially when drinking) and somewhat fickle and absent-minded. Wealth is widely (and unevenly) distributed, but the rich give freely and gambling is rampant — they are not prudent with money. Magic and the arts (arcane and traditional), combat, learning, and cuisine are all prized. **Currency:** Carasa mints its own coin stamped with the Carasian crest but is the only Trine member to value all Trine currency equally, so long as its purity is sound.

**Military.** The Carasian armies prize **chivalry, glory, and honor** over order and discipline. Drawn from the kingdom's diverse population, they fight as fierce, brave, freeform and fluid combatants, hard to predict and dangerous in sheer numbers — bolstered when structure is needed by **Dwarven axes from Moradin (the Morian Kingdom)** and the High Queen's personal guard of **elite druid-mages from the Kingdom of Parathia**. They keep no large navy and scarcely need one: the **Grand Canal** that feeds the whole inner Trine (and Gidia) runs through their territory and straight through Vallon, its walls lined with heavy ballista as a reminder of who owns the waterways.

**Learning.** Study is deeply valued. Carasa's libraries and archives rival the Luzonovian Sovereignty's — likely holding more, though far less neatly catalogued and harder to search — and are public institutions open to noble and commoner alike. Serious students enroll in well-regarded, structured universities found throughout the cities and larger towns (e.g. the Université de sept in Vallon). Common is spoken by everyone; high elven is spoken in court, old elven near Parathia, and a dwarvish-common blend near Morian.

**Royal lineage (Carasian royal line).**
1. **Anestasia the Bold** (reigned 17–93 P.T.) — Founder; brought the Morians into the kingdom in 78 P.T. (after a brief war with the Gidians) and constantly fended off the Horde Alliance.
2. **Alote the Prosperous** (93–177) — Built the first canal allowing ships to pass from the Sea/Bay of Resplendence into the Trine waters; with relative peace on the southern borders, this triggered a boom of industry, commerce, and wealth.
3. **Julien the Builder** (177–266) — Used the inherited wealth to build the capital **Vallon** into its present form, with all its major structures, and expanded the canal into the grand stone marvel it is today.
4. **Germain the Warrior** (266–422) — In 321 P.T. expanded the kingdom to include the vassal **Kingdom of Parathia**, the Carasian knights having turned the tide in the last battle of its war.
5. **Raymon the Kind** (422–448) — A kind, tolerant, just ruler and father to Beatris; killed on a tour of the southern kingdom when a hidden band of the Horde Alliance ambushed his caravan and slew everyone present.
6. **Beatris Lionsblood** (448–present) — Current ruler; strong-willed, bold, a lion in battle, sharp-tongued. After her father's murder she rode south at the head of an army and slaughtered the ambushers and more, earning her name in vengeance. (This is Jon's doc's account of the named ruler the played canon calls Queen Beatrice "the Lion's Blood.") The queen was long unmarried, married late in life and bore a son and heir, then was widowed (the king died of sickness on a voyage), and later took a partner with whom she had four daughters. (Played canon additionally records King Rupert the Young alongside her at E137; relationship to Jon's account unresolved.) Her son's arranged marriage to the Gidian princess — and the purist objection to mingling noble blood — corresponds to the played wedding storyline above.

**Provinces (Dominion).** Total population ~42,000; the capital city ~25,000.
- **Champlet (capital province)** — Northwestern, on the coast of the Bay of Resplendence and the Trine waters. An idyllic landscape of hills, fields, and forests holding the largest vineyards and finest wines of the Trine, with fishing towns along the coast. Capital city **Vallon**, "saphir sur la mer" (sapphire on the sea) — the greatest city in Alambor by Carasian boast, where all trade through Gidia flows up the canal and the primary way into the heart of the Trine. Its skyline is dominated by the gilded towers of the six deities, the **Colisée de Juli** (fighting arena), the **Halls of Wonder** (the High Queen's high court), and the **Université de sept** (university of seven). Major exports/commerce: wine, food, cotton, knowledge, and any craft or artifact imaginable.
- **Vierbonne** — Southern desert province bordering the **Kingdom of Parathia** and the **Horde Alliance**. Hot arid tropical desert giving way to full desert, stretching to Lake Mena; camel caravans and horseback travel, and Carasian settlements grow defensive and fortified near the Horde. Major town **Draçon** (pop. ~3,000). Little exports out; coastal trading and small farms sustain the people, and Parathian caravans bring spices, precious stones, and enchanted artifacts north — primarily a defensive province for the kingdom's soft southern underbelly.
- **Vierteaux** — Central province, the **breadbasket** of Carasa, bordering the **Morian Kingdom** and the kingdom's two other provinces. Gentle rolling hills, wealthy farmland, private vineyards and breweries, unwalled villages, and wagon trains flowing to the capital and major towns; to the north it butts against the Morian mountains (logging, forestry, pelts), where orcs and goblins occasionally raid down but are usually checked by the dwarves. Major town **Grelliers** (pop. ~9,000), famed for its granaries and deafening market. Major exports: food, cotton, lumber; plus expertly crafted Morian armor and weapons traded through. (Played canon's "Grellier," a city left standing late in the war, corresponds to this provincial capital.)

**Vassal states.** The **Morian Kingdom** (Scottish-flavored dwarves of Moradin; joined the Carasian Kingdom ~78 P.T. after distrusting the Gidians, providing armor, weapons, gems, and craftsmanship while relying on Carasa for food and magic) and the **Kingdom of Parathia** (Moroccan-flavored elven mageocracy worshipping the Raven Queen; became a vassal in 321 P.T. after Carasian knights saved it in the last battle of its war with Gusaash, fueling Carasa's explosion of arcane arts ever since).

**Festivals.** The Carasians love festivals and partying. Two are especially their own:
- **Carnival Amantes** — In honor of Avandra, held on the first day of Avandi (a floating day, marked by the **first bud on the Arbor Alma** and announced realm-wide by the Archavisian Order lighting purple flames). Stands in for the lost springtime after the long winter; food, drink, song, dance, trickery, and love/lust — "like Carnival in Venice with April Fools together."
- **Festival de Jeux** — A fixed five-day festival on the 25th of Tempastum, prepared for all year: games, contests, feasting, drinking, and gambling in amphitheaters and fighting pits across the kingdom; in the **Colisée de Juli** the Chief Games are held (fighting, chariot and horse racing). Category winners are announced by the High Queen herself, become realm-wide celebrities, and are immortalized along the **wall of stories** in the capital.

The kingdom also observes the realm-wide festivals (Midsommer for Erathis; Midwinter for the Raven Queen; the Feast of Vita messis at the fall of the texerine), the latter tied to the Archavisian Order's texerine harvest.

The **Harpers** (freedom fighters opposing tyranny and the slave trade) operate cells and lone agents across Alambor, including within Carasa.
