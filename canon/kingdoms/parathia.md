# Paratha

Southern, Moroccan-flavored mage-ruled land established at the Sundering settlement. Parathans are elven — a mobile nation focused on maintaining pure bloodlines — and strongly opposed the Gidian–Carasian royal wedding. Paratha is openly fine with slavery (tiefling semi-slave laborers pole its river boats while Luzonovia turns a blind eye), worships among others the Raven Queen, and its capital, sited down from the Great Rift, has struggled to get the spice out amid strange incursions and supernatural beasts coming from the Rift; hushed talk mentioned an "Alliance of the 5 Armies." Tarlif, the pre-Sundering elven mageocracy, sits in Paratha, and Berrian's home region of Vermalock lies on the Carasian–Parathean border. The cloaked White Knights who approached Berrian's mother at Moonbright seemed Parathean, from down south. Julia Vontrice serves as a diplomat here, and Jameti Costco arranged to be found at Paratha's Ancot Market Inn. The party crewed an early river journey through Parathan lands with Leon Steadyfoot (E05), and Yankee's crew later ran goods off the coast of Parathia, including magical artifacts shipping for the war effort. By the E137 war status, Paratha — like Tarlif — was under threat from the expanding Gidian empire.

## Relationships / whereabouts

## From Jon's worldbuilding docs

**Nature and rule.** Parathia is a Carasian vassal mageocracy with an Eastern (Moroccan) flavor, governed directly by spell-casters who rule as oligarchs/feudal lords. Wisdom and knowledge are prized above all, expressed through a very strict code of desert law set against an utterly free use of and constant exploration into magic. The ruling body is the **Sestet** — six mage-leaders of equal authority. Jon's prose names them **Voras, Innubine, Ibarin, Uvior, Amari, and Azahl** (this differs from the spellings the party heard for the six leaders of Tarlif — Vorace, Inobune, Abarian, Uvuor, Amari, Azal (E113); spelling conflict flagged). Several are characterized by domain and temperament: Uvior (war — "The Terse"), Innubine (history/language — "The Thinker"), and Ibarin, who also goes by **Nardif Darksky**, master of the Planes — "The rebel" (Nardif Darksi, the Nightmare King, is already a known recurring NPC; this ties his title to the Sestet seat).

**People and faith.** The dominant race is elves, with tieflings kept as indentured servants and other races present but less common (E40 letters). The accepted gods are the **Raven Queen** (primary) and **Erathis** (secondary); all other gods are banned under pain of imprisonment. The elves were once proud followers of **Corellon** amid the **eledine forests**, but when Corellon was killed in the war of the betrayer gods (the Sundering), the eledine trees and that whole region were destroyed and the elves were driven out. They became nomads who remember the past and now worship death. Their harsh laws are described as a lesson learned from watching the betrayer gods and the dynasty unfold around the Sundering. Because they live close to the **Sundering of Corellon/Torog**, the roving caravan-bands frequently venture into the **Feywild** and the **Shadowfell**.

**History and alliance.** Parathia became a vassal of the Carasian Kingdom in **321 P.T.**, after Carasian knights came to its aid in the last battle of the **War with Gusaash** (the Horde Alliance), when Parathia was on the brink of destruction. Carasia, being more tolerant of Parathian law and magic practice, has made it a fruitful alliance: Parathia exports **spices, horses, magic, and knowledge** to Carasia in return for **food and building supplies**, and this is credited with the explosion of the arcane arts within the Carasian Kingdom ever since. In return, the Carasians come to Parathia's aid against Gusaash.

**Geography and economy.** Approximate population ~12,000 — a small city of ~8,000 plus roving caravan-bands and small villages scattered throughout the desert. The capital **Tarlif** (canon **Tarlif**; Jon gives the epithet "The Rise and Fall") is the kingdom's jewel in the desert. (Per Jon's prose Tarlif is also the only seaport of an otherwise-landlocked Parathia (?) — inferred from its province description; the played canon treats the capital as sited down from the Great Rift.)

**Provinces and towns.**
- **Sarirdir** — province of the capital **Tarlif (Tarlif)**. Features the **Garden of Kathir**, a verdant "fertile crescent" paradise of wealthy plantations on rich soil, and an entrance to **the Rift**.
- **Al-Hasha** — town of **Natast**.
- **Al-Ghainah** — town of **Dubahat**.
- **Al-Wafaga** — town of **Ramaditha**; site of the Towers, including the **Tower of the Teeth** (Uvior), the **Tower of Darkness** (Innubine), and the **Shadowed Moon Tower** (Ibarin / Nardif Darksky).
- **Turandal** — town of **Tall-Fana**; the **River Alnahr** runs here.

**Bordering frontier (Carasian side).** Parathia abuts the war-torn Carasian frontier city of **Draçon** (Dracon, pop. ~3,000), a city constantly at war — high walls and tiered towers under constant repair, surrounded by barren wastes, dead grassland, and watchtowers, patrolled by wary horsemen. It blends Parathian and Carasian dress, accent, and style, and is led by the military commander and Duke **Charcon**. The arid land runs from this tropical desert south into full desert and winds around the Horde Alliance Territory and toward the colder, barren landscape around Lake Mena. Guarding the southern wastes wherever Draçon does not is the **Towers of the Teeth**: a line of walled hilltop watchtowers with massive spires and signal beacons amid scorched earth, giving a full view of the southern border and constantly fending off raiders from the southern wastes.

**Calendar/festivals (world-wide, observed here).** The world keeps 300 cycles per annum and six 50-day "sextets" (months) across Summer (Avandi, Pelius, Erathen) and Winter (Tempastum, Ravena, Morane). **Midwinter** (25th of Ravena) is the worship of the Raven Queen and the honoring of the gods lost in the Sundering — directly resonant with Parathian faith — celebrated by a journey into the wilderness with great fires, drink, and mind-altering herbs in which the spirits of the gods are said to be seen.
