# Astral Sea

The plane the party stranded themselves on at the end of E165, and the setting of E166. An
endless weightless expanse — "an ocean, but without water; a vast sea of shimmering mist,
drifting impossible stars that never seem to twinkle or change." There is no up, no down, and
no horizon. Bodies simply drift, pushed by faint currents.

Captain **Kael'vorr** of the **Silver Promise**, a **Githyanki** trader-captain who netted the
party adrift, gave them the plane's full history in E166 — the single largest cosmology
delivery of the campaign.

## Origin and the three ages

The Astral Sea was made by the gods as **the in-between place**: "when the gods had an idea
that they weren't necessarily fond of, or didn't really think fit, they tossed it, got rid of
it, threw it out." It is the bin of creation.

- **First age.** The **Githyanki** were already here — before the gods who now dwell here, as
  far as anyone knows. Their own origin is unknown even to them: "some say we were here when we
  first began; some say some of the gods created us."
- **Second age — the age of sleeping gods.** The **Sundering** is the largest event in the
  plane's history. Gods cannot die: *"They can go dormant. They can be destroyed, but not
  eradicated."* The gods destroyed in the Sundering were cast here, and over centuries their
  lingering divine essence congealed into **the God Isles**.
- **Third age — the age of the Zivens.** Roughly **3,000 astral cycles ago** the **Ziven
  Dynasty's** seven cities were ripped out of **Mena** by the gods themselves and dumped here.

## The God Isles

Each isle slowly transforms into the physical reflection of the nature of its god. Named by
Kael'vorr: **Corellon's** forests, the **crystal mountains where Bahamut rests**, the **infinite
libraries where Ioun's thoughts lingered**, and the **volcanic realms of Tiamat**.

The gods there are dormant, not gone. Some speak to mortals; some **call mortals to come to
them, heard in the back of the mind** — which is how **Malora (Melora)** reached Berrian in the
brig of the Silver Promise. Dragons, celestials, dream creatures and construct monsters have all
emerged from the isles, and the Githyanki hunt those that turn dangerous. Even the Zivens find
the isles "somewhat untouchable."

## Physical laws

- **No hunger, no thirst, no bodily needs.** Exhaustion still applies, and long rests work.
- **No time.** "There is no dawn, afternoon, night. It just is." Time passes, but nobody knows
  how it maps to the material plane. Jon confirmed in play that Alambor is in **the 500th year
  of this age**, against 3,000 astral cycles since the Zivens arrived; the party's resulting
  6:1 ratio is their own arithmetic, **not a DM ruling (?)**.
- **Movement is by mind** — 3 × Intelligence score in feet per turn. No propulsion, no swimming.
  "No map will serve you here, but your mind is everything."
- **Mass creates gravity.** Ships and cities exert a pull that strengthens with density, "like a
  tractor beam" — so the Silver Promise's decks and the streets of Ilharess have a normal "down,"
  and drifting travellers get reeled in as they approach.
- Currents run through the void and can be caught and ridden by a ship.

## Inhabitants

- **Githyanki** — natives; sailors, traders, investigators, cartographers, and peacekeepers
  between their own cities. Home city **Tu'narath**. Formal, courteous, strictly neutral about
  the Zivens.
- **Ziven Dynasty** — seven floating cities of drow, the most powerful people on the plane, who
  turned inward to study rather than conquer. City visited: **Ilharess**, the Gate of Worlds.
- **Slaad** — the Githyanki's ancient enemies, demonic creations of **Tharizdun**, who dwells
  "deep, deep, deep into the cosmos." Named but not yet encountered.
- A **leviathan-like shape with a long tail** was seen swimming in the far distance in E165.

## Getting out — color pools / fractured gates

The plane's only known exits. **Color pool** is the Githyanki name; the Zivens call them
**fractured gates**. They are tiny unstable **tears the gods left behind** when they made the
place, purposely or not. They move constantly, are **rarely more than 20 feet across**, open for
**minutes to hours**, and can collapse without warning. They cluster **near the God Isles**.
The Githyanki predict them by **tracking astral magnetism**.

They lead to Alambor, other worlds, the worlds of the gods, the celestial realms and the fey
realms. **Several known pools lead to Alambor** — Kael'vorr knows where some are — but they sit in
hard-to-reach places and are unreliable. Passage is a **"violent transition"**: no Githyanki has
ever attempted it, and the Zivens who tried were never seen again.

## E167 — "the god is the isle"

The Githyanki navigator **Ren Sark**, a fellow prisoner in the Quiet Vaults, corrected the party's
assumption that the God Isles are places where gods live:

> **The god is the isle.** … The gods, when they were sundered and sent here, **slept — and in
> sleeping, they created an entire ecosystem of themselves.** These are the God Isles. Creatures,
> beings dwell upon these isles, all manifestations of the gods that dwelt there. **So the god isle
> *is* the god.**

Further detail from the same conversation and from Jon's E167 recap of Kael'vorr's lecture:

- **The isles drift** and are spread right across the Astral Sea. Whether that is a barrier depends
  entirely on your captain: *"it depends on who you attach your sails to."*
- **Astral storms and astral winds** are a named hazard — new in E167 (Jon's recap lists "astral
  storms" among Kael'vorr's E166 syllabus). The winds around **Bahamut's** isle are *"brutal"*, and
  only certain ship captains can navigate them *"without getting taken for a pretty rough ride."*
- **Bahamut's isle** holds mountains, trees, and — by legend — **treasure**. Ren Sark was arrested
  trying to steal maps that would get him there.
- **Melora is known and present.** Asked directly: *"Yes, we know Melora."* **Tiamat** was confirmed
  present again in the same scene.
- **Astral navigation is psychic, not cartographic.** *"You don't travel the seas with physical maps
  — these are mental maps. This is the psychic waves that travel through the seas. You have to
  understand them and traverse them."* The party's charcoal map of the Rift is worthless here.
- **Observatory posts are scattered throughout the plane**, Ziven-run and restricted; one near
  Ilharess is called **Talmaq** *(spelling unconfirmed)*.
- **Divine gates** — structures "that separated the planes" — were part of Kael'vorr's E166 lecture
  per Jon's E167 recap, though the party has not investigated them.
- **Time remains unmeasurable.** Leaving the Quiet Vaults: *"the sky, the city, everything seems the
  same. You have no idea if a day has passed."*

## Relationships / whereabouts
- Contains **Ilharess** (and six other unvisited Ziven cities), **Tu'narath**, and the **God Isles**.
- Reached from Alambor by rupturing a Bag of Holding inside an extradimensional space (E165) —
  a one-way gate that cannot be reopened.
- The **Ziven Dynasty** has been trying to return to Alambor for 3,000 years and failing, while
  **Adune / Morgenrath** is reportedly close to achieving it from the Alambor side.

## Open questions

- What the **World Gate** at the centre of Ilharess is, why it is dormant, and whether it is the

- Whether the party's 6:1 time ratio between the Astral Sea and Alambor is real.
- Whether the Zivens who vanished into fractured gates died or arrived somewhere.
