# Ilharess

The Ziven city the party reached at the end of **E166** and has been inside since — a disc of
floating continent **seven miles across**, adrift in the [Astral Sea](#), carrying **over a
quarter of a million drow** and, at its centre, the dormant mile-high **[World Gate](#)** —
though see the caveat under [Open questions](#open-questions): Jon has since said the World Gate
*"was part of Ilharess in Maena, it is no more"* **(?)**.

One of **seven** Ziven cities, all of similar size and each far larger than the Githyanki capital
Tu'narath. **Named for — and belonging to — [Ilharess](#) the archmage**, a living member of
[the Hive](#) and its master of conjuration (E167). The same name is used for both, exactly as
*Izzdar* names both an archmage and his vault.

**Spelling DM-confirmed** (Jon, E166 review; he also spelled it out on air, I-L-H-A-R-E-S-S, and
used it 47 times in the library-research handout).

> *"Also known as the Gate of Worlds, or it was at some point known as that."* — Captain
> Kael'vorr, E166. **That is an old name, not a current one** (DM-confirmed).

## The city as seen

From above (E166): a **port**, a **garden quarter** of trees and rivers — *"a whole
reconstruction of nature"* — and a **grand marketplace**.

From the street (E167): broad avenues paved in **pale stone veined with glowing gold and purple**;
floating **white marble bridges** strung between support towers; **inactive portals drifting
overhead**; purple crystals for light. Perfectly clean and perfectly ordered — **no beggars, no
shouting merchants, nobody drunk** — with thousands of drow in dark blue, white and purple, each
*"the best version of what they represent."*

Jon's framing: ***"It's too perfect. It's almost odd."***

The city rides on a **floating mountain**, into which the **Quiet Vaults** are cut. Its civic
quarter holds a courthouse-like government complex, the severe **Hall of Verity**, and grander
halls whose ceilings carry **floating spheres in the constellation patterns of Alambor/Maena** and
whose walls are painted with the **history of the World Gate's construction** — all captioned in
Ziven script the party cannot read.

Non-drow are not normally admitted: *"those that come here either are to trade at the ports or to
deliver goods — other than that, it is all Zivens."* Even so, **Rhen Sarik** rates it *"a more
welcoming Ziven city than others,"* which is why Githyanki vessels dock here from time to time.

## How a terrestrial city survives with no earth under it

> **Source for this section.** Jon's between-sessions research handout (17 Aug 2026), section 3 —
> ***A City Without Earth: The Preservation and Reconstruction of Ilharess Following Exile***, by
> **Master Architect Thaelir Veyr**, Conservator of Civic Geometry, Third Architectural
> Directorate, 14th Revised Edition, **2,847 AS**. A standard student text, full of **hundreds of
> diagrams comparing Old Ilharess with the modern city.** This is the party's declared escape-route
> research, and it delivers.

Veyr's thesis: *the city was not designed for its present environment.*

> *Ilharess was raised upon Maena. Its towers possessed foundations. Its aqueducts descended
> according to gravity… Gates anticipated travelers approaching horizontally across the land.*
>
> *The first generation after the Banishment did not construct a new Ilharess. **They taught the
> old one how to survive.***

### 1. Structure — the Foundation Lattice

The Sundering took not just the surrounding land but **part of the bedrock the city stood on**.
Buildings meant to transfer weight downward suddenly ended against fractured stone open to the
Astral. The fix: **great transverse members driven horizontally through the surviving bedrock**,
binding streets, towers, foundations and old defensive walls into a **single interconnected
structure**.

> ***"The earth once carried Ilharess. Now Ilharess must carry itself."*** — Architect Melivar,
> First Reconstruction

### 2. Circulation — water that can never leave

Old Ilharess assumed downward flow and six terrestrial gates. In the Astral, none of that holds.
The ancient aqueducts were cut from their reservoirs and rerouted into **Reclamation Chambers**
beneath the central districts; water is collected, purified, condensed and pushed back up through
**ascending conduits** to elevated cisterns.

> *Many modern citizens are surprised to learn that the broad water channels visible beneath the
> eastern promenades were originally **sewers**. Their present elegance should not obscure their
> origin.*

### 3. ⭐ The Lower Works — the old city, still down there

**This is the escape-route section.** Old Ilharess had **nearly two hundred miles of service
passages**: drainage galleries, aqueduct tunnels, refuse channels, foundation galleries, servant
passages, storage vaults, **burial chambers**, and conduits carrying magical energy between public
institutions. Destroying them would have meant dismantling the city, so the architects
**repurposed** them.

Storm drains → reclamation channels. Aqueduct galleries → circulation conduits. Underground cart
roads → **maintenance corridors**. Collapsed foundations → structural inspection galleries.
Abandoned sewer junctions → **access chambers for the Foundation Lattice**.

> *Consequently, **the Lower Works preserve the street plan of Old Ilharess more faithfully than
> the city above**. A citizen walking through modern Ilharess may cross three districts without
> realizing that beneath their feet lies an avenue which once continued uninterrupted between
> them.*

And the anomalies are **not fully documented**:

> *A drainage corridor from Old Ilharess may cross beneath a modern teleportation chamber. A
> foundation gallery may terminate inside a structure constructed two millennia later. A sealed
> aqueduct may pass behind the wall of a contemporary residence. A maintenance shaft may descend
> toward what appears on modern plans to be empty space…*
>
> *Every effort has been made to document such anomalies. **No architect should claim that every
> one has been found.***

### 4. ⭐ Material Geometry vs Conjured Geometry

After the Fourth Reconstruction, permanent **spatial junctions** let distant structures behave as
neighbours. The **Archive District** is the standard example: **seven physically separate
repositories** that function internally as one complex.

So official plans distinguish two maps:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Material Geometry** | The true physical relationship between structures. |
| **Conjured Geometry** | The relationships created by permanent spatial conjunction. |

> *Failure to distinguish the two **has killed more apprentice engineers than structural
> collapse**. A doorway may lead to a chamber forty paces distant while the opposite wall of that
> chamber physically occupies another district entirely.*

### 5. ⭐ The rule that guarantees a physical way in

Wards fail, thresholds need repair, Potentium distribution can be interrupted. So:

> ***"No structure shall depend upon magic for access to the magic upon which it depends."***

Established after the failure of the Second Conjunction Network, and one of the oldest principles
of Astral civic architecture. Therefore:

> *Thus beneath **nearly every major institution in Ilharess** exists a physical service
> connection.*

These passages are narrow, plain, and reached through **maintenance doors bearing the triangular
sigil of the Civic Directorate**. They carry repair constructs, engineers, reclaimed water,
structural materials and the city's principal arcane conduits. *"They should not be confused with
public transit."*

### 6. Evacuation runs inward, and opens mechanically

> *Old Ilharess directed citizens outward… **There is no surrounding countryside anymore.***

Each district has **protected descent routes** to structurally independent **refuge chambers
embedded in the Foundation Lattice**, connected through the Lower Works so populations can move
between districts **without using public streets or conjured thresholds.** Normally sealed —
but during catastrophic failure they **may be opened mechanically from either side**, because *"no
emergency system may assume continued access to arcane power."*

> ***Magic may fail. Stone must still possess somewhere to stand.***

Veyr's closing:

> *The modern city is **ancient Ilharess concealed beneath three thousand years of adaptation**…
> descend far enough into the Lower Works and one may still walk the streets our ancestors walked
> beneath the sun. There are places below where paving stones remain worn by carriage wheels.
> There are drains that have not carried rainwater since the Sundering. There are sealed doors
> whose inscriptions identify neighborhoods that no longer exist.*

## The party's situation here

Disarmed, logged by name, and **permanently escorted** by Vaelor's Gatewatch as *"unofficially,
observers."* They have the run of the city and its libraries. Names were recorded at a reception
desk, origins declared to two inquisitors, and a self-writing slate holds a transcript of
everything said in the Hall of Verity. Examiner **Caelith Vorell** stated on the record that they
are hiding something.

**[Ilharess](#) the archmage has been told they are here and wants to meet them**, possibly with
one or two other Hive members.

Jon's closing question to the table in E167 was explicitly: ***"If you guys were going to escape,
how could you do that and where would you go?"*** — and section 3 above is his answer arriving
between sessions.

## Governance and people

- An **Inquisitorial hierarchy**: an Examiner / Senior Inquisitor, a **High Examiner** styled
  *Voice of the Second Circle*, and a High Inquisitor above that.
- A **Gatewatch** policing arrivals, under Captain **Vaelor Sythran**.
- A **Civic Directorate** owning the maintenance infrastructure (triangular sigil), a
  **Directorate of Civil Continuity**, a **Third Architectural Directorate**, and academic bodies:
  the **Collegium of Spatial Mechanics**, the **Collegium of Astral Transit**, the **Observatory
  of Boundary Phenomena**, the **Observatory of Astral Cartography**, the **Academy of Material
  Arcana**, and the **Repository of Pre-Sundering Material Culture**.
- A justice system that conjures tea for the accused and **executes Slaad off-screen**.
- Their own saying about their cities: ***"the greatest prison ever built is one where the
  prisoners no longer remember they are imprisoned."***

## The other six cities

The handout names **three more** alongside Ilharess — **Ninathar**, **Felyn'riel** and
**Omagor** — all archmage names. It never names the remaining three, and the obvious guess — that
they take the three leftover archmage names, **Izzdar**, **Zarryne** and **Valonim** — was **put to
Jon and rejected: "too neat"** (14 Sep 2026). Three of the seven cities remain unnamed.

## Open questions

- **Where are the Archive District's seven repositories physically?** Conjured Geometry means the
  party could walk between them and never cross the city.
- **Can the party get a plan?** Veyr's book contains hundreds of diagrams of Old Ilharess beside
  the modern configuration — but the party reads no Ziven script.
- Which **maintenance door** serves the library, the Hall of Verity, or the Quiet Vaults? Every
  major institution has one, by law.
- ⚠️ **Is the World Gate actually gone?** In the E167 review Jon defined three separate things:
  *"the world gate was part of illharess in Maena, **it is no more**, the color pools are rifts
  between planes here in the Astral sea and the divine gates are where the god isles are."* But
  the party saw a dormant mile-high gate structure at the city's centre in E166 and E167, and the
  civic complex carries the **history of the World Gate's construction** on its walls. Possibly the
  *working* gate is no more and what stands is a dead monument — **not asserted**, needs a ruling **(?)**.
- **Where does the World Gate structure sit in the Lower Works?** The city's centre is a mile-high gate;
  the architectural history quoted here never mentions it.
- The **burial chambers** among the old subterranean systems are listed once and never explained.
